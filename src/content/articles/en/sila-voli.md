Somewhere over the last forty years, one word received a disproportionate promotion. We began using *willpower* to explain nearly everything that happens between a person and a plate — and nearly everything that doesn't. We hung this quality on something immeasurable in the part of us that can't be seen, and blamed those who seemed to lack it.

I want to take that word apart, carefully, because it has cost many of us many years.

## The code is rules you never agreed to.

Here is what a code is: from inside it, it doesn't feel like a rule. It feels like a fact. *Of course* a smaller body is better. *Of course* you should have stopped after one. By the time you heard the rule clearly, you had already been obeying it for decades.

My daughter Irina grew up watching me argue with my plate. She's thirty-one now and eats without argument, which I think is the only inheritance that mattered. I don't want to make a tidy story out of this. It wasn't tidy. But there was a year — I was fifty-four — when I stopped counting, and the first thing to change wasn't the food.

> "I thought the problem was the loud rules about food. It turned out to be the quiet ones."
>
> — A reader, 47, in a letter

## Discomfort is not danger.

The first thing to change was the phrase inside. The voice that had told me for forty years *"you should know better"* gradually grew quieter. Not because I argued with it. Because I stopped agreeing with it.

Most of what we call a lapse is a small piece of unbearable information that the body brings to feeling, asking to be felt. When we eat through that feeling, food is not a failure. Food is the rescue.

## A quieter phrase.

You can take this one: *This is not a failure of character. This is a body responding to an old code.* You don't have to believe it the first time. It's enough to want to test it.

I've been testing it for ten years. The code is quieter. The plate is quieter. I am, mostly, quieter. None of this is transformation. All of it is relief.

---

*It's not the willpower you lack. It's the code — inherited, mostly unspoken — telling you what counts as a good day, a good plate, a good body. The code is loud. You are not the problem.*
