There was a year when I barely thought about food. Not because I stopped eating or changed anything about what I ate — but because the internal conversation about it became less frequent. At first I didn't notice. You usually only notice the noise, not its absence.

Then I noticed. It was a strange feeling — not joy, not victory. Just space that hadn't been there before. A morning without the first thought about what was "allowed" today. An evening without a final tally.

## What changed, and what didn't.

Not much changed. Even more didn't. The old reactions didn't go anywhere — they just stopped being automatic. A pause appeared between the impulse and the action, and sometimes a different choice fits inside that pause.

That year wasn't a turning point. There was no single moment, no single book, no single conversation. There was a slow accumulation of quiet — a little at a time, imperceptibly, until one day I found myself on the other side of something I never found a name for. Just quieter. That was enough.
