There are men we usually don't reach. Not because they don't read — but because books about food and the body were not written for them. They were written for a different audience, in a different language, with a different assumption about who suffers and what it looks like.

These men are quiet. They're fifty, sixty. They have rules about the body — hard, early, never said out loud. They received those rules from fathers who were also silent. And they pass them on — not because they want to, but because they don't know another way.

## Control as a language of love.

For many men of our generation, control over the body was the language of self-respect. Don't eat too much, don't show weakness, hold it together. This is not pathology — it's culture. But culture can become outdated, and what once helped you hold together can now get in the way of simply living.

Grief, exhaustion, anxiety — we have all of those too. We just learned not to call it food, and not to call food that. Two parallel silences that never meet. This letter is an attempt to bring them together.

## What you can do with this tonight.

I'm not offering a plan. I'm offering one question you can ask yourself this evening: what rule about food and the body did I receive without choosing it? Just name it. Don't change it. Don't judge it. Just — hear it.

Sometimes that's enough to make the code a little quieter. Not gone — but no longer the only truth.
