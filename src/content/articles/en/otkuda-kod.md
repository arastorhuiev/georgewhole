The rules about food don't begin with us. They begin at a long table in another decade, in another kitchen, with someone who didn't choose them either — who simply took them in like air and passed them on. This is not an accusation. It's just how transmission works.

Our mothers and fathers did with food what their culture, their poverty or abundance, their own shame and their own anxiety did. They didn't sit down and invent rules for us. They just ate the way they knew how — and we watched.

## The rule that was never called a rule.

The code doesn't look like a body of law. It looks like an ordinary evening. A mother who doesn't take seconds. A father who says "earn it" — in passing, without thinking. A grandmother who feeds with love while counting every bite at the same time. All of it accumulated, layer by layer, into what later feels like character.

The code is not guilty and not cruel. It's just old. And now that we can hear it, we get to decide — whether to carry it forward or not. That, really, is the whole choice.
