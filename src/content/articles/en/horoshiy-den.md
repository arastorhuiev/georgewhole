There is one rule that almost no one says out loud, because it seems too simple to be a rule. It goes roughly like this: a day when you ate right is a good day. A day when you didn't is a bad one. And this small verdict happens quietly, every evening, just before sleep.

The problem isn't that the rule is cruel. The problem is that it takes an entire day — with its conversations, its work, its tiredness, its small pleasures — and reduces it to a single tally at the plate. A great deal gets lost that way.

## The price we pay in silence.

When a "good day" is defined by food, the body becomes the judge of everything else. Anxiety grows not from something bad happening — but from eating the wrong thing. It's a quiet, constant labor that almost no one notices in themselves until they stop.

Stopping doesn't mean stopping eating consciously. It means stopping grading the day by the plate. A day can be hard and still be good. The body can ask for something unplanned — and that's fine too. A good day is a day that was lived. Everything else is detail.
