I stopped counting at fifty-four. Not because I made some decision. Just one day I realized I was tired — not of the food, but of the counting. Of the daily verdict I had been quietly, methodically passing on myself for as long as I could remember sitting at a table.

The first thing to change wasn't the food. That matters, because I was waiting for the food to change — and for a long time I didn't notice that something was already happening. What changed was the phrase inside. The voice that said "you should know better" became a little quieter. Not gone — it just stopped being first.

## No "before" and "after."

I won't describe what I was like before and what I became after. Not because I'm hiding anything — but because that frame is itself part of the old code. "Before/after" is a promise of transformation. What happened to me is not transformation. It's slow, nonlinear relief.

Ten years is a long time. Things went differently at different points. The old voices came back during hard stretches. Sometimes I heard the count again. But the threshold shifted: now I notice it faster, and the pause between the voice and agreeing with it has grown longer. That, as it turns out, is enough.

## What changed in the kitchen.

Irina — my daughter — now eats beside me without tension. That is the only change I count as real. Not because she "learned" something right from me — but because I stopped playing out the argument in front of her. Quietly, without any pedagogy. I just stopped.

This is not a tidy story. I don't recommend any particular path. I only say that it's quieter on this side — and that the word "quieter" turned out to matter more than I had thought.
